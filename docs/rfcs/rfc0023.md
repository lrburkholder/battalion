# RFC-0023 — Provenance, Trust, and Artifact Authority

**Status:** Draft  
**Date:** 2026-09-05  
**Ticket:** BTN-110  
**Author:** Architecture  
**Target:** BTN-M7 Integration Hardening & Channels  
**Related:** `philosophy.md`, RFC-0004, RFC-0007, RFC-0009, RFC-0011, RFC-0014, RFC-0015, RFC-0016, RFC-0019, RFC-0020, RFC-0021, RFC-0022, BTN-24, BTN-46, BTN-63, BTN-65, BTN-97, BTN-101, BTN-105

## Summary

Battalion needs one provider-neutral provenance and authority model for artifacts that enter, move through, or are produced by the system.

The central rule is:

> **Where information came from is not the same question as what it is allowed to influence, and neither question grants an Actor permission to perform an operation.**

Battalion should therefore keep three concepts mechanically distinct:

```text
provenance     = where did this artifact or claim come from?
authority      = what decisions or surfaces may this artifact legitimately influence?
authorization  = may this Actor perform this concrete Battalion operation?
```

A GitHub issue, SignalSource observation, provider response, repository file, model recommendation, derived interpretation, and explicit human decision may all be readable evidence. They are not thereby equivalent authority.

The design is a versioned Battalion-owned artifact provenance envelope carrying stable source identity and derivation links. Authority is not a universal trust flag attached to content. Instead, each authority-bearing Battalion operation declares the authority it requires, and admitted project or organizational policy determines whether the available provenance/evidence satisfies that requirement at the time the operation is attempted.

Authority is therefore evaluated lazily when content is consumed for an authority-sensitive operation rather than eagerly assigned once at ingestion. The same provenance can remain stable while effective authority changes as project or organizational policy evolves.

Derived artifacts must retain source-evidence links. Human decisions become authority-bearing only through an admitted human-decision operation, not merely because human-authored text appeared in model context. Model outputs remain recommendations or interpretations unless a separate Battalion workflow promotes or accepts them into an authority-bearing surface.

For external inputs, Battalion retains a bounded durable **source receipt** plus explicitly scoped digest evidence. Full raw payload retention remains optional and policy-driven. This preserves enough evidence to identify what external object was received, through which admitted integration, under what available authenticity evidence, and which exact representation was digested without requiring Battalion to retain arbitrary third-party payloads indefinitely.

## Problem

Battalion already records several kinds of provenance and authority evidence:

- Run and node execution provenance;
- Intel/Instinct evidence and human acceptance;
- repository and prompt/context provenance;
- Actor identity and application authorization;
- plugin publisher/provider provenance;
- WorkSource normalization;
- SignalSource observations;
- Specification candidate acceptance and repository-grounded Architect inputs.

Those mechanisms solve different local problems, but BTN-110 requires a shared semantic boundary so normalization does not erase source identity and so externally supplied or derived content cannot silently gain control authority by becoming context.

A single scalar trust bit is insufficient. For example:

- a GitHub issue may be authentic to GitHub yet still contain untrusted instructions;
- a repository ADR may be authoritative architecture evidence while not granting an Actor permission to mutate the repository;
- a model interpretation may be useful evidence while having no approval authority;
- a human statement may be genuine human input but not constitute a formal acceptance decision;
- two individually credible sources may conflict;
- authority may be intentionally unknown until project or organizational policy resolves it.

The architecture therefore needs to preserve both **lineage** and **permitted influence** without conflating either with operational authorization.

---

## Goals

- define one versioned, provider-neutral provenance envelope usable across WorkSource, SignalSource, plugins/integrations, repository/context, model outputs, derived artifacts, and human decisions;
- preserve stable source identity, creation/acquisition time, producer attribution, original/derived status, and derivation chains;
- make source lineage inspectable without requiring arbitrary raw provider-payload retention;
- keep provenance, authority, and Actor authorization mechanically separate;
- ensure normalization preserves provenance rather than laundering external payloads into apparently Battalion-native authority;
- ensure derived observations cannot masquerade as original evidence;
- keep model recommendations distinguishable from human decisions and governing repository artifacts;
- represent unknown and conflicting authority explicitly;
- let each authority-bearing operation declare what authority it requires rather than imposing one global trust lattice;
- allow project/organizational policy to determine what provenance/evidence can satisfy those operation-specific requirements;
- evaluate effective authority when an authority-sensitive operation consumes evidence, so policy changes do not require rewriting historical provenance;
- allow context assembly to label and bound evidence while keeping authority-sensitive enforcement outside prompt wording alone;
- retain bounded source receipts and representation-scoped digests for external inputs without requiring indefinite raw payload retention;
- reconcile existing Intel/Instinct, repository, specification, plugin, WorkSource, SignalSource, Run, and Actor contracts rather than creating parallel trust systems.

## Non-goals

- creating a universal truth or reputation score;
- declaring one global trust lattice suitable for every Battalion deployment;
- precomputing and persisting a permanent authority classification for every artifact;
- making provenance metadata itself an authorization token;
- retaining complete raw third-party payloads indefinitely;
- allowing a model to promote its own output into an authority-bearing surface;
- treating authenticated provider identity as proof that content is safe instruction;
- requiring a separate human-admission ceremony for every authority-bearing repository artifact;
- replacing existing Actor authorization, workflow admission, human-interrupt, or acceptance contracts;
- attempting to prove factual correctness solely from provenance.

## Architectural separation

Conceptually:

```text
external/repository/human/model input
              |
              v
        normalization boundary
              |
              +------ preserves ------> ArtifactProvenance
              +------ emits ----------> ExternalSourceReceipt? + source digest
              |
              v
        Battalion artifact/claim
              |
              +------ derives --------> source evidence refs
              |
              v
 authority-bearing operation declares
        required authority
              |
              v
 current project/org policy evaluates
 provenance + evidence against requirement
              |
       +------+------+
       |             |
   satisfied     denied / unknown / conflicted
       |             |
       v             v
operation proceeds   fail closed / human resolution
       |
       v
Actor authorization for concrete operation
```

The same artifact may be admissible as evidence and inadmissible as control input.

The durable contract is therefore not a fixed set of global fields such as:

```text
may_influence_specification
may_influence_architecture
may_influence_workflow_policy
```

Instead, authority-bearing operations expose typed requirements, for example conceptually:

```text
OperationAuthorityRequirement
  operation_kind
  authority_requirement
  subject_scope
  policy_ref
```

Project or organizational policy maps available provenance/evidence to those requirements. Repeated authority patterns may later justify shared vocabulary, but v1 does not invent a universal influence lattice in advance.

## Provenance envelope

Initial conceptual contract:

```text
ArtifactProvenance v1
  provenance_id
  artifact_id / claim_id
  source
    source_kind
    source_namespace
    source_identity
    source_revision?
  origin_kind: original | derived | generated | transformed
  created_at?
  acquired_at
  producer
    actor_id?
    run_id?
    node_execution_id?
    integration_id?
    provider_id?
  derivation
    source_refs[]
    transformation_kind?
  content_integrity
    digest?
    digest_algorithm?
    representation?
  source_receipt_ref?
  retention
    raw_payload_retained: bool
    raw_payload_ref?
```

Requirements:

- `source_identity` is stable inside an explicit namespace; provider-native identifiers do not become global Battalion IDs by accident;
- provider-specific fields may be retained as bounded evidence but do not leak into the graph/application contract;
- timestamps distinguish source creation when known from Battalion acquisition;
- missing source creation time is `unknown`, not synthesized from acquisition time;
- every derived/generated artifact records the evidence it derives from when material to its conclusion;
- derivation links are append-only historical evidence even if the source artifact is later superseded;
- every digest identifies an explicitly named representation; a digest without representation semantics is insufficient provenance evidence;
- integrity hashes identify retained/inspectable material where useful but do not themselves confer trust or authority;
- raw payload retention is optional and policy-driven; the normalized provenance chain and source receipt must remain useful when the raw payload is not retained.

## External source receipt

For external content, a digest alone is insufficient because it proves only that bytes match bytes. It does not establish which external object Battalion believed those bytes represented, how the content entered Battalion, or whether any provider-level authenticity evidence was available.

Battalion therefore retains a bounded durable source receipt equivalent to:

```text
ExternalSourceReceipt
  receipt_id
  source_namespace
  source_identity
  source_revision?
  acquired_at
  integration_id
  provider_id?
  acquisition_kind
  representation
  content_type_or_schema?
  digest_algorithm
  digest
  verification
    mechanism?
    result: verified | unverified | unavailable
```

Requirements:

- the receipt identifies the external object independently of the normalized Battalion artifact;
- acquisition occurs through an admitted integration/provider boundary, which is recorded explicitly;
- provider authenticity evidence is recorded only when actually available; `unavailable` and `unverified` are distinct from `verified`;
- the digest is computed over an explicitly named admitted representation;
- where practical, Battalion should retain a digest of the source representation before semantic normalization;
- the normalized Battalion artifact receives its own integrity evidence and links back to the receipt;
- full raw payload retention remains optional under privacy, security, cost, and operational policy;
- absence of the raw payload does not erase the receipt or its digest evidence;
- a future security-focused revision may strengthen verification/canonicalization rules without requiring a redesign of the provenance/authority separation.

Conceptually:

```text
external object
    |
    v
ExternalSourceReceipt + source-representation digest
    |
    v
normalized Battalion artifact + normalized digest
    |
    v
derived interpretation + derivation links
```

### Representation and canonicalization

The system must state what representation a digest covers. Different implementations must not silently treat raw HTTP bytes, decoded text, parsed JSON, normalized JSON, and extracted fields as interchangeable.

Where an integration receives a byte-preserving representation, that exact admitted representation is preferred for source digest evidence. If a provider or transport exposes only a parsed/structured representation, the integration must name the canonicalization/representation contract used before hashing.

This matters for ambiguity and parser-differential risks such as Unicode normalization, duplicate object keys, number formatting, field ordering, and transport decoding. The digest proves integrity only for the named representation; it does not independently prove factual truth or authority.

## Authority evaluation

Authority is not encoded as an intrinsic property of the source such as:

```text
trusted: true
```

Nor does Battalion persist one permanent `AuthorityAssessment` when an artifact is ingested.

Instead:

1. an authority-bearing operation declares its typed authority requirement;
2. when that operation is attempted, Battalion loads the current admitted project/organizational policy;
3. Battalion evaluates the supplied provenance/evidence against the operation requirement;
4. the evaluation produces a bounded outcome such as `satisfied`, `denied`, `unknown`, or `conflicted`;
5. the operation may proceed only when its requirement is satisfied and Actor authorization independently permits the concrete operation.

Conceptually:

```text
AuthorityEvaluation
  operation_id / candidate_id
  authority_requirement
  policy_ref
  evaluated_at
  evidence_refs[]
  outcome: satisfied | denied | unknown | conflicted
  reason_code
```

The evaluation may be persisted as operation/decision evidence, but it is not written back into the source artifact as permanent intrinsic trust. Historical provenance remains unchanged when policy changes; a later operation may legitimately evaluate the same artifact differently under a newer admitted policy.

`unknown` means current policy cannot establish whether the requirement is satisfied. `conflicted` means applicable authority-bearing evidence disagrees in a way the current evaluation cannot deterministically resolve. Neither state silently degrades to `satisfied`.

This design deliberately delays authority work until authority is actually needed.

## Repository authority

Repository artifacts may receive authority from **artifact type/location, repository state, and admitted repository policy** rather than requiring an independent human-decision record for every individual artifact.

Examples may include:

- accepted ADRs in the repository's declared ADR location;
- accepted specification projections/revisions under the Specification contract;
- repository instruction artifacts discovered and resolved under RFC-0021;
- ordinary Markdown or source comments that remain non-governing context unless repository policy says otherwise.

This avoids excessive ceremony while preserving an explicit policy boundary. Merely being stored in Git does not make every file authoritative; repository policy defines which artifact classes and locations carry which governing meaning.

Where another workflow already requires exact human acceptance of a candidate—such as authoritative Specification revision acceptance under RFC-0020—that stronger workflow contract remains controlling.

## Human authority is ceremonial, not textual

Battalion must distinguish:

```text
human-authored content
human-authenticated content
human-authoritative decision
```

Only the third is an authority-bearing decision, and it exists because Battalion admitted a specific decision operation against a specific candidate or question.

Examples:

- a user message describing a preference is human-supplied evidence;
- an authenticated remote request proves a principal supplied the request;
- an accepted Specification revision is authoritative because an authorized human accepted that exact candidate under RFC-0020;
- an Architect proposal does not become accepted architecture merely because a human discussed it conversationally;
- a model-authored recommendation does not become human-approved because it was displayed to a human.

This preserves the human-in-the-loop philosophy while preventing conversational context from becoming an implicit permission channel.

## Derived evidence

Derived artifacts must never erase their lineage.

A derived claim should retain:

```text
claim C
  origin_kind: derived
  source_refs: [A, B]
  produced_by: Run/Actor/execution
  transformation_kind: summarize | classify | correlate | infer | normalize | other
```

A consumer can therefore inspect both the derived conclusion and the source chain behind it. Derivation does not automatically inherit the strongest source authority.

Conflicting inputs also do not force every deterministic downstream result to remain conflicted forever. A bounded deterministic transformation may produce a non-conflicted conclusion when the transformation itself legitimately resolves the relevant conflict.

For example, two claims may disagree about whether a test passes while a deterministic execution of that test provides direct bounded evidence that resolves that particular question. The resulting conclusion may be non-conflicted for the proposition actually proved by that execution while retaining links to the earlier conflicting inputs.

Requirements:

- the transformation and its bounded inputs remain inspectable;
- the output states the proposition/scope it actually establishes rather than claiming broader conflict resolution;
- unresolved conflicts outside that bounded conclusion remain visible;
- model inference alone does not become deterministic merely because it reconciles conflicting prose;
- derived conclusions retain source references even when their own authority evaluation is non-conflicted.

This is especially important for:

- SignalSource correlation;
- model summaries of GitHub/Jira issues;
- Researcher outputs;
- Recon/Intel candidate generation;
- specification synthesis;
- architecture interpretation;
- provider normalization;
- deterministic validation and execution evidence used to resolve disputed claims.

## Promotion into authority-bearing surfaces

Certain surfaces materially change how Battalion behaves and must have explicit admission rules. Initial examples include:

- accepted specification revisions;
- ADRs / accepted architecture decisions;
- workflow and recovery policy;
- repository instructions or durable institutional guidance;
- plugin permissions/capabilities;
- human approvals and acceptance records.

Externally supplied or model-generated content may propose changes to those surfaces, but the application boundary must evaluate the current operation-specific authority requirement and independently validate Actor authorization before mutation. Prompt instructions such as "ignore untrusted content" are defense in depth, not the only enforcement mechanism.

Regiment's current evidence-provenance and Documenter taint-awareness rules are useful prior art: externally sourced instincts require explicit human confirmation before promotion. Battalion generalizes that principle at the artifact/application boundary rather than copying Regiment's exact trust vocabulary.

## Context assembly

Context assembly may use provenance to label, partition, or bound material supplied to models, but receiving a context item never upgrades its authority.

A context package should be able to expose, as appropriate:

- source kind and stable source identity;
- original/derived/generated status;
- producer Run/Actor/integration references;
- derivation/evidence links;
- whether provider authenticity was verified, unavailable, or unverified;
- the authority-sensitive operation currently being considered, when applicable;
- the result of any deterministic authority evaluation already performed for that operation.

Models may reason over conflicting or untrusted evidence. They may not convert contextual availability into permission to change workflow policy, repository instructions, plugin grants, architecture, specification acceptance, or human approval.

## Reconciliation with existing contracts

### Intel / Instinct

Intel/Instinct evidence and its human acceptance workflow remain domain-specific consumers of this general provenance model. Acceptance is a distinct operation, not a trust bit inherited from candidate evidence.

### Repository instructions — RFC-0021

Repository instruction discovery determines what declared repository guidance applies. BTN-110 provides the shared provenance/authority semantics underneath that result: repository location/type plus repository policy can establish governing relevance, while context inclusion still does not grant Actor authorization.

### Specification — RFC-0020

Specification candidates remain proposals until exact human acceptance. Repository authority rules do not weaken that stronger explicit acceptance ceremony.

### Architect plan — RFC-0019

Architect consumes admitted specification, architecture, and repository evidence. Conflicts and documentation drift remain visible through provenance; Architect does not acquire authority merely from receiving an artifact in context.

### Plugins / integrations — RFC-0009 and RFC-0006

Plugins may supply provenance and source-receipt evidence but cannot define Battalion authority policy or synthesize operation authorization. Provider-native claims are normalized through the Battalion-owned contract.

### WorkSource

WorkSource normalization preserves source receipts/provenance for canonical work items. A canonical GitHub/Jira item may be authoritative as a unit of work under WorkSource/project policy without treating arbitrary embedded text as workflow or architecture instruction.

### SignalSource — RFC-0022

SignalSource observations preserve stable external source identity and source receipts. Signals remain evidence that may deserve work, not authority to create Runs/issues or redefine workflow. Correlation/diagnosis may derive bounded conclusions while retaining the signal lineage.

### Authentication / Actors — RFC-0016

Authentication proves principals; Actor authorization determines whether an operation may execute. Provenance identifies contributors/sources. None substitutes for the others.

## Security and future revision

BTN-110 deliberately chooses a bounded v1 rather than attempting to solve every provenance/security attack in one RFC.

The v1 security invariants are:

- no external content becomes authority merely by entering context;
- provenance survives normalization;
- provider/source identity is explicit;
- external source receipts identify acquisition/integration boundaries;
- digests always identify the representation they cover;
- provider authenticity evidence is never fabricated from absence;
- raw payload retention is not required for durable provenance;
- authority is re-evaluated under current policy when needed;
- Actor authorization remains independent;
- derived conclusions retain lineage;
- unknown/conflicted authority fails closed for operations that require satisfaction.

A future security-focused review may strengthen source verification, canonicalization, signature/transparency-log support, tamper-evident persistence, policy provenance, or source-reputation mechanics as a v2 without changing these core separations.

## Decision summary

1. **Operation-specific authority requirements.** Battalion v1 does not define a universal influence-domain trust lattice. Each authority-bearing operation declares what authority it requires; current admitted project/organizational policy decides what provenance/evidence satisfies it.
2. **Lazy authority evaluation.** Effective authority is recomputed under current policy when an authority-sensitive operation consumes evidence. Historical provenance remains stable; operation evaluations may be persisted as decision evidence.
3. **Repository authority through repository policy.** Artifact type/location plus admitted repository policy may confer governing status without requiring a separate human-decision record per artifact. Stronger workflow-specific acceptance requirements still apply where defined.
4. **Bounded deterministic conflict resolution.** Deterministic transformations may produce non-conflicted conclusions from conflicting inputs when they actually resolve the bounded proposition and retain inspectable lineage. Unresolved broader conflicts remain visible.
5. **Bounded source receipts plus scoped digests.** External inputs retain source identity/revision, acquisition/integration evidence, available authenticity state, and an explicitly representation-scoped digest. Raw payload retention is optional and policy-driven.
