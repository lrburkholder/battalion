# RFC-0028 — Anchored Artifact Annotations and Revision-Aware Human Review

**Status:** Draft  
**Date:** 2026-09-07  
**Ticket:** BTN-122  
**Author:** Architecture  
**Target:** Post-v2 workflow orchestration  
**Related:** `philosophy.md`, BTN-30, BTN-43, BTN-58, BTN-108, BTN-109, BTN-113, BTN-120, RFC-0015, RFC-0018, RFC-0026

---

## Summary

Battalion should define a provider-neutral human-review primitive for attaching structured feedback to an exact durable artifact revision and a bounded location within that revision.

The primitive has two related but separate records:

1. **annotations** record comments, questions, revision requests, and other anchored human feedback;
2. **review decisions** record authority-bearing acceptance, rejection, or other admitted dispositions when current Actor capability and policy permit them.

Neither record mutates the reviewed artifact or prior review history.

Every review is revision-aware. An annotation always identifies the exact artifact revision that the human saw. A later artifact revision may attempt to relocate the anchor for display or resolution tracking, but the original annotation remains bound to the original revision. An authority-bearing decision never silently carries forward to a newer revision.

The design supports text, structured artifacts, and diff-like artifacts without treating line numbers as stable identity. Anchor resolution is type-aware and produces explicit states such as `exact`, `relocated`, `ambiguous`, or `missing`.

Battalion owns review persistence, authorization, revision checks, workflow routing, and durable history. Desktop and future clients call the application boundary. Presentation code does not write review persistence directly.

This gives Battalion a precise human-review mechanism that can participate in RFC-0015 typed workflow boundaries and RFC-0026 bounded revision loops without reducing human oversight to a generic approve/reject prompt.

---

## Problem

Battalion already treats human judgment as a first-class authority boundary. The current architecture also increasingly depends on durable, revisioned artifacts rather than conversational context.

Those two principles need a common review mechanism.

A generic confirmation prompt is insufficient for review of artifacts such as:

- specifications;
- architecture plans;
- ADR proposals;
- review findings;
- implementation diffs;
- Cartography artifacts;
- structured workflow artifacts.

A human often needs to say something more precise than "approve" or "reject":

- this paragraph is unclear;
- this requirement changes scope;
- this finding is accepted but deferred;
- this element needs revision;
- this part is approved, but another part is unresolved;
- this review comment applied to revision 4, not revision 5.

If Battalion stores that feedback only in chat prose, later workflow stages must scrape language and guess what the human meant.

If Battalion stores only one artifact-level boolean, it loses location, reviewer disagreement, partial review, unresolved findings, and revision history.

If Battalion reuses an annotation against a later artifact without checking revision identity, it can falsely claim that a human reviewed content they never saw.

Battalion therefore needs a durable review contract with explicit artifact identity, anchors, Actor identity, revision semantics, authority checks, and workflow integration.

---

## Goals

- Define a versioned review contract independent of PySide6, Markdown, model provider, and artifact type.
- Bind every annotation to an exact durable artifact revision.
- Support bounded anchors for text, structured artifacts, and diffs.
- Treat line numbers and presentation coordinates as hints, not durable identity by themselves.
- Preserve original annotations and decisions as immutable historical evidence.
- Detect when a newer artifact revision makes an annotation stale.
- Permit type-aware anchor relocation for inspection without rewriting historical review evidence.
- Represent multiple reviewers and conflicting review outcomes explicitly.
- Represent superseding comments and resolved/unresolved findings explicitly.
- Support partial review without flattening an artifact into one boolean state.
- Keep non-authority comments separate from authority-bearing decisions.
- Require Actor capability and current policy checks for approval or rejection where those operations carry authority.
- Prevent model-authored annotations from satisfying human-authority requirements by themselves.
- Feed review outcomes through RFC-0015 typed boundaries.
- Route revision loops through RFC-0026 and failure/escalation through RFC-0018 where applicable.
- Reference BTN-120 independent-review findings without allowing human annotation or the producing role to mutate the original reviewer evidence.
- Expose review operations through the application boundary for desktop and future clients.
- Keep the first implementation local-first and compatible with Battalion's existing durable artifact model.

## Non-goals

- Build a general collaborative document editor.
- Define rich-text rendering or editor widgets.
- Require browser loopback or a local HTTP server.
- Make every annotation authority-bearing.
- Let a model grant itself approval by emitting structured output.
- Reopen or rewrite old artifact revisions.
- Automatically merge reviewer disagreements into one verdict.
- Make fuzzy anchor relocation authoritative.
- Define one universal anchor algorithm for every artifact type.
- Replace BTN-120 independent-review evidence.
- Replace RFC-0015 workflow contracts or RFC-0026 iteration policy.
- Persist raw chain-of-thought as review evidence.

## Design principles

### 1. Review targets exact revisions

The review target is not merely an artifact ID.

Conceptually:

```text
ReviewedArtifactRef
  artifact_id
  artifact_kind
  revision
  content_digest
```

An annotation created against revision `R4` remains an annotation against `R4` forever.

If `R5` exists later, Battalion may determine whether the anchor appears to correspond to content in `R5`, but it does not rewrite the annotation target to `R5`.

This distinction is required for auditability and for truthful human approval evidence.

### 2. Annotation and authority-bearing decision are separate concepts

A human comment is evidence of human review activity. It is not automatically an authorization decision.

Conceptually:

```text
ReviewAnnotation
  annotation_id
  schema_version
  artifact_ref
  anchor
  actor_id
  created_at
  originating_run_id?
  annotation_kind
  body?
  related_evidence_refs[]
  supersedes_annotation_id?
```

Representative annotation kinds are:

```text
COMMENT
QUESTION
REQUEST_REVISION
INFORMATION
UNRESOLVED
```

Authority-bearing review outcomes use a separate record:

```text
ReviewDecision
  decision_id
  schema_version
  artifact_ref
  actor_id
  created_at
  originating_run_id?
  disposition
  coverage
  annotation_refs[]
  authorization_evidence_ref
  policy_evidence_ref
  supersedes_decision_id?
```

Representative decision dispositions are:

```text
ACCEPT
REJECT
REQUEST_REVISION
UNRESOLVED
```

`REQUEST_REVISION` and `UNRESOLVED` may appear in both annotation and decision contexts. Their meaning differs by boundary:

- an annotation states feedback at an anchor;
- a decision states the admitted review outcome for the declared coverage.

The serialized vocabulary can be refined during implementation. The architectural rule is that ordinary comments must not gain authority merely because they use a structured disposition word.

### 3. Models may propose review structure but cannot manufacture human authority

A model may:

- suggest an anchor;
- summarize review comments;
- propose a candidate disposition;
- connect a comment to related evidence.

A model-authored record cannot satisfy a workflow requirement for human approval, rejection, or another authority-bearing human decision.

Battalion must check:

- Actor identity;
- Actor capability;
- applicable project/workflow policy;
- exact artifact revision;
- decision scope or coverage;
- any required human confirmation.

Unknown or insufficient authority fails closed.

### 4. Historical review records are append-only

Resolving a finding does not edit the original annotation.

Superseding a comment does not delete the original comment.

Review history is an event chain or linked immutable record set.

Examples:

```text
annotation A1: REQUEST_REVISION on revision R1
artifact revision R2 created
annotation A2: COMMENT on R2, supersedes A1
resolution E1: A1 resolved by R2 and A2
```

The application may project a current status such as `resolved`, but the source records remain inspectable.

### 5. Anchor relocation is evidence, not authority

Relocation exists to help humans understand whether older feedback still appears relevant after revision.

It does not silently transfer review authority.

Conceptually:

```text
AnchorResolution
  source_annotation_id
  source_artifact_ref
  candidate_artifact_ref
  status
  resolved_anchor?
  method
  evidence
```

Representative statuses are:

```text
EXACT
RELOCATED
AMBIGUOUS
MISSING
```

A newer artifact revision also makes any decision bound to the previous revision stale unless an admitted policy explicitly defines narrower unaffected coverage and the exact new candidate is re-confirmed as required.

The safe default is:

```text
artifact revision changed
    -> prior authority-bearing decision does not apply to new revision
```

## Anchor model

Battalion should use a tagged anchor family rather than one universal coordinate system.

Every anchor has:

```text
Anchor
  anchor_kind
  artifact_revision
  identity_payload
  integrity_evidence
  presentation_hint?
```

`presentation_hint` may include line, row, column, path, hunk number, or UI coordinates. It is not sufficient identity by itself.

### Text anchors

A text anchor should identify the selected text and enough bounded surrounding context to detect movement or ambiguity.

Conceptually:

```text
TextAnchor
  selected_text_digest
  selected_text_length
  prefix_context_digest?
  suffix_context_digest?
  occurrence_hint?
  line_range_hint?
```

The artifact revision and content digest remain the primary review identity.

For the exact revision, the anchor should resolve deterministically.

For a later revision, Battalion may search for a uniquely matching selected fragment plus bounded context. If several candidates match, the result is `AMBIGUOUS`. If none match, it is `MISSING`.

Battalion must not choose the nearest line number and call it the same anchor merely because the line moved.

### Structured artifact anchors

Structured artifacts should prefer stable element identity supplied by the artifact contract.

Examples include:

```text
JSON Pointer / typed field path
stable element_id
schema-defined node identity
```

A structured anchor can include a digest of the referenced value or subtree.

Conceptually:

```text
StructuredAnchor
  element_identity
  value_digest?
  schema_version?
```

If the element still exists but its value changed, Battalion can report that distinction instead of pretending the exact reviewed content is unchanged.

### Diff anchors

Diff review must bind to the exact compared revisions.

Conceptually:

```text
DiffAnchor
  base_revision
  head_revision
  path
  side
  selected_change_digest
  source_range_hint?
  target_range_hint?
```

A line number in a diff is not stable enough by itself because the diff identity depends on both endpoints and the selected change content.

If either endpoint changes, the previous diff review is historical evidence against the previous diff candidate.

### Artifact-level anchors

Some review decisions apply to the complete artifact rather than one element.

Battalion should support an explicit whole-artifact anchor instead of encoding this as an empty range.

```text
ArtifactAnchor
  scope: WHOLE_ARTIFACT
```

The decision still binds to one exact artifact revision.

## Review coverage and partial approval

A review decision must state what it covers.

Representative coverage forms are:

```text
WHOLE_ARTIFACT
ANCHORS [A1, A2, ...]
ELEMENT_SET [E1, E2, ...]
```

Partial acceptance therefore means:

```text
ACCEPT coverage=[specified anchors/elements]
```

It does not imply that unreviewed content is accepted.

Battalion should expose the distinction between:

- accepted coverage;
- rejected coverage;
- revision-requested coverage;
- unresolved coverage;
- unreviewed coverage.

A client may show an aggregate status for convenience, but the durable model must not discard the underlying set.

---

## Multiple reviewers and conflicts

Review records are per Actor.

Battalion must not collapse:

```text
Reviewer A: ACCEPT
Reviewer B: REQUEST_REVISION
```

into a synthetic boolean such as `approved=false` without preserving both source decisions.

Workflow policy decides what the conflict means for progression.

Examples may include:

- all required reviewers must accept;
- one designated authority decides after advisory comments;
- any rejection requires revision;
- conflicting authority-bearing decisions require HumanInterrupt.

That policy belongs to the admitted workflow and authority model, not the annotation store.

---

## Supersession and resolution

Annotations and decisions may link to older records through explicit supersession or resolution evidence.

Battalion should distinguish:

```text
superseded  -> a later record replaces the reviewer's current statement
resolved    -> the underlying concern was addressed or otherwise disposed
stale       -> artifact revision changed; prior review does not describe the current candidate exactly
missing     -> an anchor cannot be located in a candidate later revision
ambiguous   -> an anchor has more than one plausible later location
```

These states are not interchangeable.

A stale annotation can remain unresolved.

A resolved annotation remains historical evidence.

A superseding annotation does not erase the prior record.

---

## Relationship to BTN-120 independent review evidence

BTN-120 owns the independent-review trust boundary.

Anchored human review may reference a BTN-120 finding, but it must not rewrite that finding.

Conceptually:

```text
IndependentReviewFinding F1
        |
        +--> ReviewAnnotation A1: "accepted; revise this section"
        |
        +--> ReviewDecision D1: REQUEST_REVISION
```

The original finding remains in the independent-review evidence store or artifact family owned by BTN-120.

Human disposition is separate evidence.

The reviewed/producing role must not gain mutation authority over independent-review findings through this RFC.

## Workflow integration

### RFC-0015 typed boundaries

Review results should cross workflow stages as Battalion-owned typed references.

A receiving stage should consume a contract such as:

```text
ArtifactReviewInput
  artifact_ref
  annotation_refs[]
  decision_refs[]
  unresolved_refs[]
```

It should not scrape the reviewer's chat transcript.

A revision stage can therefore receive exact structured requests and produce a new artifact revision that records which review items it addressed.

### RFC-0026 bounded iteration

The initial motivating flow is:

```text
candidate artifact R1
        ↓
human anchored review
        ├─ ACCEPT R1 -> admitted success
        ├─ REQUEST_REVISION -> revision stage
        │                        ↓
        │                   artifact R2
        │                        ↓
        └──────────────── human review of exact R2
```

Each revision is a new artifact revision.

Prior annotations remain attached to prior revisions.

A later round may explicitly resolve or supersede prior findings, but it cannot rewrite them.

Round bounds and exhaustion remain owned by RFC-0026.

### RFC-0018 failure and escalation

Cases such as these route through admitted failure/escalation policy where appropriate:

- required reviewed artifact cannot be resolved;
- an authority-bearing decision fails authorization;
- an anchor is malformed for the target artifact;
- required review evidence is unavailable;
- iterative review reaches its configured bound;
- conflicting required authorities cannot be reconciled by current workflow policy.

A review client does not invent a recovery path.

## Application boundary

Presentation code must not mutate review persistence directly.

The application boundary should expose operations equivalent to:

```text
create_annotation(...)
read_review(...)
create_decision(...)
resolve_annotation(...)
supersede_annotation(...)
resolve_anchor_against_revision(...)
list_review_history(...)
```

The exact method names are implementation detail.

The boundary must enforce:

- artifact and revision resolution;
- schema validation;
- Actor identity;
- capability and policy checks for authority-bearing decisions;
- append-only history rules;
- stale-revision checks;
- reference integrity;
- independent-review evidence custody rules;
- Run/workflow linkage where applicable.

Desktop, CLI, and future clients receive projections from this boundary.

---

## Desktop interaction boundary

The first desktop design should remain intentionally small.

A client needs to support:

1. open an exact durable artifact revision;
2. select text or a structural element, or choose the whole artifact;
3. create a comment/question/revision request at that anchor;
4. create an authority-bearing review decision when permitted;
5. show whether an annotation is current, stale, relocated, ambiguous, resolved, or missing;
6. show prior annotations and decisions without rewriting them;
7. open a newer revision and see which old anchors can be safely located for inspection.

The architecture does not require an embedded browser, localhost callback server, or ECC-style Plan Canvas implementation.

PySide6 may provide one presentation surface. It does not define the durable contract.

---

## Persistence

Review evidence should follow Battalion's existing durable artifact/evidence persistence conventions rather than create a presentation-owned store.

At minimum Battalion must persist:

- review/annotation identity;
- exact artifact revision identity;
- anchor payload;
- Actor identity;
- timestamp;
- originating Run/workflow where applicable;
- related evidence references;
- supersession/resolution links;
- authority/policy evidence for authority-bearing decisions.

Deleting or superseding an artifact from the current workflow view must not delete historical review evidence while normal project retention rules still require it.

---

## Revision rules

The following invariants should be mechanically testable.

### Exact revision

If an annotation targets artifact `A` revision `R`, Battalion can reproduce which durable revision the human reviewed.

### Artifact changed

If `A:R+1` exists, Battalion does not report an authority-bearing decision on `A:R` as approval of `A:R+1`.

### Moved anchor

If selected content moves unchanged and resolves uniquely under the type-specific relocation rule, Battalion may display `RELOCATED` for inspection.

The source annotation remains attached to the old revision.

### Changed anchor

If the referenced content changed materially, the new revision requires new human review for authority-bearing progression.

### Deleted anchor

If the content no longer exists, relocation reports `MISSING`.

### Ambiguous anchor

If more than one candidate location is plausible, relocation reports `AMBIGUOUS` and requires human judgment if the relationship matters.

Battalion must not pick one silently.

---

## Security and authority considerations

Anchored review creates a high-value authority boundary because an approval may permit downstream execution.

The implementation must therefore prevent:

- forging Actor identity;
- applying a decision to a different revision;
- changing decision coverage after authorization;
- converting advisory model output into human approval;
- editing old review records in place;
- changing an anchor after a decision has been created;
- using relocation heuristics to transfer approval to modified content;
- allowing the producing role to mutate independent-review evidence referenced by a human annotation.

Authority-bearing decisions should include sufficient immutable candidate identity for Battalion to detect any post-decision mutation.

---

## Testing requirements

Credential-free tests should cover at least:

- annotation against an exact artifact revision;
- artifact revision changes after annotation;
- stale prior approval after artifact revision;
- unique relocated text anchor;
- deleted anchor;
- ambiguous relocated anchor;
- structured element anchor with changed value;
- diff anchor tied to exact base/head revisions;
- multiple annotations on one artifact;
- multiple reviewers with conflicting decisions;
- partial acceptance coverage;
- unauthorized Actor approval denial;
- model-authored approval candidate failing human-authority requirements;
- request-revision transition into a bounded iteration round;
- accepted exact revision;
- superseding annotation history;
- resolution without mutation of original annotation;
- durable review history after artifact supersession;
- reference to BTN-120 review finding without mutation permission;
- application-layer enforcement independent of presentation client.

## Candidate sequence

```text
Artifact A:R1
   |
   +--> Human selects anchor X
   |       |
   |       +--> Annotation A1: REQUEST_REVISION
   |
   +--> ReviewDecision D1: REQUEST_REVISION
           |
           v
   RFC-0026 revision transition
           |
           v
Artifact A:R2
   |
   +--> resolve A1 against R2
   |       -> RELOCATED / MISSING / AMBIGUOUS
   |
   +--> Human reviews exact R2
           |
           +--> Annotation A2 (may supersede/resolve A1)
           |
           +--> ReviewDecision D2: ACCEPT A:R2
                   |
                   v
            admitted downstream boundary
```

At no point does `D1` become approval of `R2`.

## Rejected alternatives

### Store review only as chat messages

Rejected because workflow stages would need to parse prose and historical artifact identity would be weak.

### Use line numbers as the durable anchor

Rejected because lines move as text changes and structured artifacts may not have meaningful lines.

### Mutate annotations when a new revision appears

Rejected because this destroys truthful historical evidence.

### One artifact-level approve/reject boolean

Rejected because it cannot represent partial review, anchored findings, conflicts, multiple reviewers, or unresolved items.

### Treat every structured annotation disposition as authority-bearing

Rejected because comments and model-authored suggestions must not gain approval authority accidentally.

### Automatically carry approval forward when anchors relocate

Rejected because relocation is not proof that the human reviewed the new artifact revision.

### Copy ECC's browser-loopback Plan Canvas

Rejected. Battalion should reuse the interaction principle, not the implementation architecture.

## Open questions

1. **What should be the first durable artifact identity used by the implementation?**  
   RFC-0015 defines the conceptual `ArtifactRef`, but BTN-122 implementation may arrive before one universal artifact registry exists. Should the first implementation require a common Battalion artifact resolver, or support a small adapter interface over existing durable artifacts?

2. **How much text should a text anchor persist?**  
   A full selected fragment makes review easy to inspect but can duplicate sensitive or large artifact content. Digests plus bounded excerpts reduce duplication but make display dependent on resolving the historical artifact. The architecture should set a privacy- and audit-safe default.

3. **Should relocation ever produce `EXACT` on a newer revision?**  
   One option is to reserve `EXACT` for the original revision and use `RELOCATED` for any later revision even when the selected fragment is byte-identical. That is simpler and makes revision boundaries visible.

4. **What is the minimum partial-approval policy for v1?**  
   The contract can represent partial coverage, but workflow policy may initially permit authority-bearing `ACCEPT` only for the whole exact artifact. This would keep the data model extensible while avoiding ambiguous downstream semantics in the first implementation.

5. **Who may resolve another Actor's annotation?**  
   Resolution can mean "the concern was addressed," not "the original reviewer agrees." Battalion should distinguish workflow resolution evidence from reviewer confirmation so one Actor cannot silently speak for another.

6. **Should stale annotation relocation run automatically or on demand?**  
   Automatic relocation improves UI feedback but may add unnecessary work for large artifacts. On-demand resolution is simpler and keeps relocation a projection rather than part of artifact persistence.

7. **Which authority contract should own the exact capability names for review decisions?**  
   BTN-122 should reuse existing Actor/capability policy rather than invent `can_approve_artifact` locally. The RFC should name the operation semantics without freezing premature implementation identifiers.

---

## Decision criteria

Before acceptance, this RFC should establish agreement on these points:

- annotation and authority-bearing decision remain distinct;
- every decision is bound to one exact durable artifact revision;
- no approval silently transfers to a newer revision;
- anchors are artifact-type aware and line numbers are hints only;
- relocation is non-authoritative projection evidence;
- historical records are append-only;
- partial review is represented explicitly even if v1 authority policy remains whole-artifact only;
- independent-review findings remain immutable under BTN-120 custody;
- workflow routing uses existing typed boundary, bounded iteration, and failure/escalation architecture;
- desktop remains a client of the application boundary rather than the owner of review persistence.
