# RFC-0012 — Deterministic Workflow Admission and Tactician Escalation

**Status:** Accepted  
**Decision Date:** 2026-08-28  
**Ticket:** BTN-131  
**Author:** Architecture  
**Target:** Post-v2 workflow orchestration  
**Related:** `philosophy.md`, BTN-26, BTN-29, BTN-45, BTN-120, BTN-133, BTN-134, RFC-0008, RFC-0010

---

## Summary

Battalion should support **proportionate workflows**, but it should not invoke an AI Tactician merely to classify work that available project evidence can classify mechanically.

The accepted architecture is **deterministic-first workflow admission with Tactician as a semantic uncertainty/escalation role**:

1. Battalion assembles bounded admission evidence and evaluates deterministic workflow policy.
2. Deterministic admission produces one of three conceptual outcomes: **full required**, **compact admissible**, or **uncertain**.
3. Full-required work does not invoke Tactician merely to confirm policy.
4. Compact-admissible work may be offered directly to the human without a model call.
5. Only uncertain work is escalated to Tactician for an independent semantic assessment.
6. The human makes the consequential workflow choice. Tactician does not own workflow authority.
7. Once execution starts, an upgrade-only ratchet prevents new risk or ambiguity from being hidden by the originally selected recipe.

Battalion deliberately does **not** compute a numeric task-complexity score. Mechanical facts correlate with complexity and risk but cannot reliably reduce semantic difficulty, uncertainty, architectural consequence, or hidden work to a scalar before implementation. Deterministic policy answers the narrower question: do known authoritative facts require full handling, positively admit compact handling, or leave the choice semantically uncertain?

For a genuinely trivial, well-specified Hello World change in an understood repository, Battalion should normally offer compact execution without spending a Tactician model call. For an apparently small change whose semantic or architectural consequence cannot be established mechanically, Tactician and the human effectively perform bounded **planning poker**: independent assessments are compared, and disagreement is useful evidence rather than something to average away.

Tactician is not a LangGraph implementation node and does not construct arbitrary workflows. Registered, versioned `WorkflowRecipe`s and deterministic application policy remain authoritative.

## Problem

Battalion's original fixed workflow intentionally favored safety and explicit role separation:

```text
Architect -> Driver -> Reviewer -> Refactorer -> Reviewer / completion
```

CLI UAT on BTN-129 exposed the cost of applying maximum-strength orchestration to trivial work. A Hello World exercise caused substantial model-dependent deliberation through stages whose marginal value was low for that task.

The tempting response is to estimate task complexity and select a workflow from that estimate. That fails because mechanically visible size is not semantic complexity. A one-line boolean change may alter an authorization boundary; a forty-file mechanical rename may be conceptually trivial. File count, line count, dependency count, affected symbols, and similar metrics are useful evidence but not a trustworthy scalar measure of difficulty or risk.

Making an AI Tactician the mandatory classifier for every ticket also merely moves fixed overhead earlier in the workflow. If Battalion already has enough evidence to know that a task is eligible for compact execution, asking a model to say "this looks simple" is ceremony.

The architecture therefore separates mechanically established facts, semantic uncertainty, workflow authority, and the human's consequential workflow choice.

## Goals

- make small, well-specified tasks materially cheaper and faster than complex architectural work;
- avoid spending Tactician tokens where deterministic evidence is sufficient;
- preserve the full workflow as safe default/fallback;
- use deterministic evidence for hard prohibitions and positive compact eligibility;
- use Tactician specifically where semantic uncertainty remains;
- make human/Tactician disagreement inspectable and useful;
- define finite, versioned workflow recipes rather than model-generated graphs;
- require explicit human approval before using a recipe that omits default safeguards;
- upgrade automatically to more conservative handling when risk or ambiguity is discovered;
- allow better Cartography/project knowledge to reduce Tactician invocation over time.

## Non-goals

- producing a universal numeric task-complexity score;
- treating diff size, file count, estimated duration, or dependency count as sufficient proof that work is simple;
- invoking Tactician for every work item;
- allowing Tactician to invent arbitrary graphs or make product/architecture decisions;
- allowing compact execution to bypass verification, independent review, authorization, write scope, side-effect policy, secrets policy, or required human interrupts;
- duplicating Specifier or Architect responsibilities;
- optimizing solely for fewer model calls.

---

## Why not a deterministic complexity score?

Battalion can deterministically observe or derive useful signals: repositories/domains apparently affected, paths and symbols, dependency relationships from Cartography, sensitive interfaces/persistence/auth/migration surfaces, governing specifications/ADRs, available tests/gates, known write scope, project policy, and eventually historical evidence from similar work.

Those facts are useful individually. Collapsing them into `complexity = files + domains * 5 + ...` creates false precision. Complexity includes uncertainty, hidden work, semantic consequence, institutional knowledge, and architectural significance. Planning poker exists partly because engineers with different knowledge can reasonably assess the same apparently bounded task differently.

Battalion's advantage is that Cartography, repository policy, accepted specifications/ADRs, and durable evidence can remove **some** uncertainty deterministically before asking a human or model to reason about what remains. Deterministic policy therefore classifies **admission evidence**, not task complexity.

## Deterministic admission outcomes

```text
WorkflowAdmissionAssessment
  assessment_id
  work_item/spec revision
  policy_version
  evidence_refs[]
  outcome:
    FULL_REQUIRED
    COMPACT_ADMISSIBLE
    UNCERTAIN
  reasons[]
  hard_risk_flags[]
  missing_evidence[]
  admissible_recipe_ids[]
```

### Full required

Known facts prohibit reduced execution: for example a new architectural boundary, policy-required cross-system impact, public interface/schema changes, persistence migrations, authorization/secrets/privacy concerns, high-consequence deployment/release behavior, or another configured full-only surface. Tactician cannot override these facts and need not be invoked merely to confirm them.

### Compact admissible

Positive evidence establishes that compact may be offered. This does not prove the task objectively "easy"; it proves the deliberately narrow compact admission contract is satisfied. Initially that requires explicit desired behavior, an existing governing architecture, bounded expected scope, no known full-workflow prohibition, known behavioral verification, required deterministic tests/gates, and retained independent review.

A sufficiently explicit Hello World task should normally land here.

### Uncertain

Neither full prohibition nor positive compact eligibility can be established. Examples include ambiguous architectural consequence, partially known scope, possible new policy/ownership boundaries, disagreement between ticket and Cartography evidence, missing institutional context, or mechanically small but semantically consequential work. This is Tactician's primary territory.

## Architectural boundary

```text
human work item / approved specification
              |
              v
     bounded admission evidence
              |
              v
   deterministic admission assessment
       /             |             \
 FULL_REQUIRED  COMPACT_ADMISSIBLE  UNCERTAIN
       |             |                 |
       |             |                 v
       |             |          Tactician assessment
       |             |                 |
       +-------------+-----------------+
                     |
                     v
               human decision
             /       |       \
          compact   full   clarify/cancel
             |       |
             v       v
       admitted WorkflowRecipe
                     |
                     v
      WorkflowExecution / ImplementationRun
```

The application layer owns admission evidence, deterministic policy, registered recipes, human actions, Actor authorization, Run creation, persistence/resume, graph dispatch, cost policy, and upgrade transitions. Tactician owns semantic assessment only when deterministic policy reports uncertainty.

## Registered workflow recipes

Recipes are versioned policy artifacts, not prompt-generated node lists.

```text
WorkflowRecipe
  recipe_id
  recipe_version
  workflow_kind
  stages[]
  mandatory_verification[]
  independent_review_requirement
  human_interrupt_policy refs
  eligibility_policy refs
  upgrade_triggers[]
```

The then-current full Implementation Run remains default/fallback.

The initial compact recipe is:

```text
Driver implementation
  -> required RED/GREEN or equivalent behavioral evidence
  -> configured deterministic tests / quality gates
  -> independent review
  -> completion / human acceptance
```

It normally omits separate Architect and proactive Refactorer model passes plus redundant intermediate model review, but retains write-scope enforcement, behavioral verification, gates, independent review, required interrupts, authorization/side-effect policy, provenance, and cost policy. BTN-134 owns the durable semantic Review Run architecture.

Clarification is not a weaker implementation recipe; it is refusal to admit implementation until missing intent, architecture, authority, or evidence is supplied by the human, BTN-45 Specifier, Architect, or bounded Recon/Cartography as appropriate.

## Tactician as uncertainty escalation

Tactician is invoked when deterministic policy returns `UNCERTAIN`, not as a mandatory toll booth before every Run.

Its bounded input contains the work-item/spec revision, deterministic assessment and reasons, accepted specification/ADRs when present, bounded Cartography/Recon evidence, known scope, recipe summaries, mandatory policy/gates, and prior explicit human constraints.

Its output is an independent semantic assessment:

```text
TacticianAssessment
  assessment_id
  recommended_recipe_id | clarification
  rationale[]
  risk_flags[]
  missing_evidence[]
  input_evidence_refs[]
  model/prompt/config provenance
```

Tactician may recommend compact, full, or clarification, but cannot override a hard prohibition or create a new recipe. Raw provider reasoning is not persisted or treated as acceptance evidence.

---

## Human + Tactician as bounded planning poker

When deterministic evidence runs out, the human and Tactician effectively perform a specialized form of planning poker. They do not assign story points, average scores, or vote on authority. Tactician supplies an independent assessment from bounded project evidence; the human supplies engineering judgment and may possess institutional context not represented in Battalion.

Agreement can make the choice straightforward. **Disagreement is itself valuable evidence.** For example:

```text
Human:     Compact — this is configuration plumbing.
Tactician: Full — this appears to introduce a new model-selection policy
                  boundary not governed by accepted architecture.
```

The useful question is not "who wins?" but **what evidence or context explains the disagreement?** The human remains decision authority. Missing human context should ideally become a durable artifact, Cartography constraint, ADR/specification, or explicit policy rather than a repeated private correction. Repeated Tactician recognition of a stable condition should similarly motivate better deterministic policy/evidence where appropriate.

```text
uncertain work
    -> human/Tactician assessment
    -> agreement/disagreement + decision + outcome
    -> missing knowledge/policy identified
    -> Cartography/spec/ADR/policy improved
    -> similar future work becomes deterministically classifiable
```

A healthy mature project should therefore reduce the fraction of routine work requiring Tactician without pretending semantic uncertainty can be eliminated.

## Human approval

A reduced workflow requires explicit approval by an authorized human Actor unless a future human-authored project policy establishes standing approval semantics.

For deterministic compact admission, Battalion shows evidence rather than inventing a Tactician explanation:

```text
Compact workflow is available.

Evidence:
✓ desired behavior is explicit
✓ existing architectural boundary applies
✓ affected scope is bounded
✓ no configured full-workflow prohibition detected
✓ required verification is available

Runs: Driver -> verification/gates -> independent review
Skips: Architect, proactive Refactorer

[Use Compact] [Use Full] [Clarify] [Cancel]
```

For uncertain work, the surface additionally shows Tactician's assessment and risk flags. The human may always choose full. This RFC does not create a mechanism for compact execution to override deterministic `FULL_REQUIRED` policy.

---

## Upgrade-only ratchet

Compact execution upgrades to clarification/full when acceptance criteria prove ambiguous; Driver needs a new architecture decision; scope crosses an unanticipated boundary; interface/schema/persistence/migration/integration behavior changes; auth/secrets/privacy/security becomes material; required verification cannot execute trustworthily; gates fail beyond compact remediation; independent review finds a material concern; writes exceed admitted scope; or another configured full-only condition becomes true.

Upgrade is application policy, not a Tactician decision. There is no automatic downgrade.

---

## Role relationships

**Specifier (BTN-45)** determines what Battalion is being asked to build/change when product intent is ambiguous. Not every explicit ticket requires Specifier. Tactician may not invent missing specification content.

**Architect** owns material design decisions. Tactician may recognize that architecture is unresolved but cannot answer the architecture question. Compact is valid only where existing architecture governs material choices.

**Cartography / deterministic analysis** supply evidence such as domain/path bindings, symbol/dependency relationships, cross-cutting constraints, sensitive surfaces, governing artifacts, and verification mechanisms. Better evidence should reduce unnecessary Tactician calls.

**Reviewer / BTN-134** supplies independent semantic assurance. Compact reduces redundant deliberation; it does not erase independent review.

---

## Persistence, resume, model and failure semantics

Admission assessments and optional Tactician assessments are pre-admission evidence, not Implementation Runs. An admitted execution references the admission assessment, optional Tactician assessment, recipe/version, approving Actor, approval evidence, work-item/spec revision, policy version, and admitted risk flags.

Resume uses the admitted recipe and persisted upgrade state; Tactician is not rerun merely because a Run resumes. Materially stale inputs require re-admission or stronger handling, never silent downgrade.

Only uncertain cases incur Tactician model cost. Tactician participates in normal model identity and usage evidence but does not select its own provider/model.

Missing/contradictory deterministic evidence never becomes compact eligibility. Tactician failure cannot authorize compact execution; the human may choose full where intent is sufficiently clear or return to clarification.

## Alternatives considered

### Mandatory pre-run Tactician — rejected

This was the initial RFC draft. It correctly separated recommendation from authority but paid a model call when Battalion already had sufficient evidence. Tactician should add semantic judgment, not ceremony.

### Deterministic numeric complexity score — rejected

Mechanical size/risk signals cannot reliably represent semantic complexity, hidden work, architecture consequence, or uncertainty. A scalar threshold creates false precision.

### Deterministic three-way admission + Tactician uncertainty escalation — selected

This spends probabilistic reasoning only where deterministic project knowledge runs out while preserving independent semantic judgment for ambiguous cases.

### Deterministic policy + direct human choice, no Tactician — viable fallback

This remains the benchmark Tactician must beat. If evaluation shows human choice from deterministic evidence performs as well as Tactician-assisted uncertain cases, remove the role.

### Tactician as graph node — rejected

Selection occurs before workflow admission; graph placement would blur Run identity and encourage probabilistic output to become authority.

### Always full / generic fast mode — rejected

Always-full is unnecessarily expensive for bounded work; generic fast mode hides omitted assurances.

## Evaluation plan

Build a credential-free corpus covering trivial Hello World changes, small regression-tested bugs, docs/config cases, deceptively small security changes, schema/interface changes, cross-domain changes with few edited lines, ambiguous features, refactor-only work, policy-forced full work, and semantically uncertain cases.

Record deterministic outcome/evidence, whether Tactician was invoked, its recommendation, human choice and disagreement reason, later upgrades, reviewer catches/escaped defects, retries/interrupts, elapsed time, model tokens/streamed-character counts, and operator correction effort.

Track:

```text
% FULL_REQUIRED deterministically
% COMPACT_ADMISSIBLE deterministically
% UNCERTAIN -> Tactician
% Tactician recommendations overridden by human
% compact executions later ratcheted upward
```

The main questions are whether deterministic-first admission achieves required assurance with less cost/latency/operator effort, and whether Tactician materially improves decisions or exposes useful missing context on `UNCERTAIN` cases. If Tactician does not beat deterministic evidence plus direct human choice, remove it.

## Accepted decisions

1. Battalion supports registered, versioned workflow recipes.
2. Full remains default/fallback.
3. Battalion does not compute a universal task-complexity score.
4. Deterministic admission produces full-required, compact-admissible, or uncertain.
5. Tactician is primarily an uncertainty escalation role, not a mandatory pre-run role.
6. Tactician is advisory, not workflow authority and not a LangGraph execution node.
7. The human remains the consequential workflow decision-maker.
8. Compact retains Driver behavioral proof, deterministic verification/gates, and independent review while normally omitting separate Architect and proactive Refactorer passes.
9. Compact eligibility depends on positive specification/architecture/scope/risk evidence, not mechanical size alone.
10. Execution ratchets only toward stronger handling.
11. Human/Tactician disagreement is useful evidence that should feed durable project knowledge or policy when possible.
12. Battalion should reduce unnecessary Tactician invocation as project evidence improves.
13. Tactician remains removable if evaluation shows no material benefit over deterministic admission plus direct human choice.
