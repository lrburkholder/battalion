# ADR-0016 — Make Instinct Promotion an Audited Human Boundary

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-08-13

## Implementation status

Implemented by BTN-23 and BTN-34. Durable pre-review candidate evidence and its
decision-backed inbox are merged.

## Context

Recon emits untrusted candidate Instincts and has no authority to publish them.
The Intel repository accepts only validated `AcceptedInstinct` records, but the
transition between those contracts needs an explicit human decision. Editing a
candidate must not rewrite Recon's original output or its creation provenance,
and rejection must never make content retrievable as knowledge.

The decision itself is durable evidence of the authority transition. Without a
separate record, an accepted Instinct proves who accepted it but cannot
distinguish unchanged acceptance from operator-edited acceptance or prove that
a candidate was rejected.

## Decision

Introduce an operator review workflow outside the execution graph. For each
`CandidateInstinct`, an operator chooses exactly one of `accept`,
`edit-and-accept`, or `reject`.

Acceptance constructs a new validated `AcceptedInstinct`, attaches human
acceptance provenance, and stores it through the immutable Intel repository.
Edit-and-accept may replace only knowledge-content fields; lifecycle, schema,
creation provenance, and acceptance provenance are controlled by the workflow.
The input candidate is never mutated. Rejection performs no Intel write.

BTN-34 persists the original candidate separately as create-only Markdown at
`<project>/.battalion/recon/candidates`. Review disposition is a projection of
the independent decision record and never a mutation of that file. Rejected
candidates are retained indefinitely as audit evidence; no candidate deletion
operation is provided.

Every choice creates a separately versioned, append-only decision record with
the action, operator, timestamp, candidate identifier, and resulting accepted
identifier when applicable. One immutable decision is allowed per candidate
identifier. The workflow remains separate from `RunState`, and neither Recon
nor the execution graph receives promotion authority.

## Consequences

- Human authorization is explicit and auditable for accepted, edited, and
  rejected candidates.
- Rejected candidates cannot appear in active or historical Intel retrieval.
- Operator edits preserve the original Recon creation provenance while the
  decision record identifies that editing occurred.
- Review decisions and accepted Instincts use local append-only persistence;
  interactive CLI presentation remains a caller concern.
