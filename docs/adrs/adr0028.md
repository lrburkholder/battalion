# ADR-0028 — Authorize Battalion operations, not identities or transports

**Status:** Accepted  
**Date:** 2026-08-26  
**Ticket:** BTN-64  
**Related:** RFC-0007, RFC-0011, ADR-0025, ADR-0026, BTN-60, BTN-61, BTN-63, BTN-65, BTN-68, BTN-70

## Context

Battalion now has durable Actor identity, and future external integrations will
be able to resolve provider identities to those Actors. Identity resolution is
necessary for attribution but cannot safely imply authority. The same human
operation may originate from desktop, CLI, REST, a notification action, or a
future bidirectional integration, and those transports must not acquire
different authorization semantics.

Provider permissions are also unsuitable as Battalion authority. A provider may
permit an action that Battalion project policy forbids. Conversely, Battalion
may authorize an operation that a provider later rejects. Those are different
decisions at different boundaries.

## Decision

Battalion authorization is evaluated at the shared application boundary against
stable Battalion operation identifiers and Battalion-owned target context.

Resolving a caller to an `Actor` establishes identity only. Before a protected
operation executes, the application layer evaluates the Actor, operation,
project, target, baseline project capabilities, and applicable Battalion-owned
contextual policy. Presentation clients and provider adapters do not make the
authoritative allow/deny decision.

Policy is transport-neutral. Desktop, CLI, REST, notification actions, and
external integrations map user intent to the same Battalion operation before
authorization. Provider-specific roles, scopes, credentials, API methods, and
MCP tool names do not enter the core authorization vocabulary.

Effective authority follows an intersection model. Battalion invariants and any
future organization ceiling may be narrowed by project, Actor, and target
policy; lower scopes cannot widen a higher-scope denial. Assignment and
ownership may constrain policy but do not grant capabilities by themselves.

`recon.accept` and `recon.reject` remain separate authorization operations.
`membership.grant` and `membership.revoke` likewise remain separate; grant is
privilege-widening and may require stricter delegation policy than revocation.

Initial default contextual constraints apply to `ticket.assign`,
`ticket.owner.transfer`, and `membership.grant`. Assignment and ownership
operations enforce target project eligibility, while membership grants cannot
exceed the caller's delegable authority or a higher-scope ceiling. Other
operations gain contextual restrictions only through explicit policy.

Denied protected operations produce an explicit authorization outcome and do
not partially mutate protected Run, worker, Intel, assignment, ownership,
membership, or external-provider state.

BTN-64 does not introduce a parallel security log. RFC-0006/BTN-65 defines the
minimum evidence semantics for integration capability operations, and BTN-70
owns durable external side-effect attempt/outcome and idempotency evidence.
BTN-60 may initially return typed authorization decisions for local/internal
operations. A future general security-event journal, if required, should be a
single deliberate cross-cutting contract rather than a second audit model hidden
inside authorization.

Security-sensitive authority changes require an appropriately authorized human
Actor. AI roles, model output, external identity resolution, provider admin
status, assignment, ownership, or possession of a broad provider credential are
not sufficient to widen Battalion authority.

## Consequences

- BTN-60 project capabilities become inputs to a reusable operation-level policy
  boundary rather than presentation-client permission checks.
- BTN-61 consumes explicit contextual rules for assignment and ownership.
- BTN-63 external identity mappings may resolve `actor_id` but cannot grant an
  operation.
- BTN-65 defines integration evidence expectations; BTN-70 implements durable
  external side-effect evidence/idempotency rather than BTN-64 inventing a
  competing security log.
- BTN-68 and later integration policy can reuse the same operation identifiers
  regardless of REST, MCP, SDK, webhook, or other transport.
- Compound actions such as interrupt resolution plus Run resume must satisfy all
  required authorization before the first protected mutation.
- Provider-side authorization failures remain distinguishable from Battalion
  policy denial.
- Future organization policy can be added as a higher-scope ceiling without
  changing the meaning of existing project capabilities.
- Application invariants such as Run lifecycle, write scope, stale-write
  protection, and worker constraints remain independently enforced after an
  authorization allow.

## Alternatives considered

### Identity resolution implies authority

Rejected because it collapses identity and authorization and makes external
account linking a privilege grant.

### Provider permissions are Battalion permissions

Rejected because it couples domain authority to providers and allows an
over-privileged credential to bypass Battalion policy.

### Each client enforces its own permissions

Rejected because clients would drift and a new adapter could bypass policy.

### Assignment or ownership grants authority

Rejected because responsibility transfer must not be privilege escalation.

### Generic Recon decision operation

Rejected. Accept and reject have different policy and evidence meaning and
remain separate operation identifiers.

### Generic membership management operation

Rejected. Grant and revoke remain distinct because granting widens authority and
may require stricter policy.

### AI roles may approve authority changes

Rejected because authority-changing decisions are a human governance boundary.

## Implementation status

This ADR records the accepted BTN-64 architecture. Project membership/capability
persistence and concrete application checks remain implementation work owned by
BTN-60 and consuming tickets. RFC-0011 defines the operation vocabulary,
decision contract, precedence, contextual defaults, compound-operation rule,
evidence ownership, and required tests.
